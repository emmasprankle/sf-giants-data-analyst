# SF Giants Pitching — 2024 Season Overview

*Sources: FanGraphs 2024 leaderboards, Baseball Reference 2024 team pitching, Baseball Savant expected stats.*

## Organizational Identity

The Giants' pitching philosophy centers on three structural pillars: **ground ball generation**, **walk suppression**, and **park exploitation**. These are not independent choices — they form a coherent system. Ground balls stay in the infield, avoiding Oracle Park's deep outfield and suppressing extra-base damage. Low walk rates minimize base traffic without requiring elite strikeout rates. And Oracle Park's cold marine air, large foul territory, and deep power alleys amplify the value of each.

This philosophy produces pitchers who win with contact management rather than swing-and-miss. The 2024 staff was built around it, with ground ball rates well above MLB average (~44%) across the top of the rotation and bullpen.

## 2024 Rotation Construction

The rotation split into two tiers by production:

**Tier 1 — Proven contributors**
- Logan Webb (SP): 204.2 IP, 4.4 fWAR — franchise anchor, only qualified starter
- Blake Snell (SP): 104.0 IP, 3.1 fWAR — mid-season acquisition, elite strikeout profile

**Tier 2 — Development/depth arms**
- Kyle Harrison (SP): 124.1 IP, 0.8 fWAR — left-handed homegrown arm, HR/FB rate concern
- Jordan Hicks (SP): 109.2 IP, 0.5 fWAR — high-velocity sinker, transitioning to starter role
- Hayden Birdsong (SP): 72.0 IP, 0.1 fWAR — rotation depth, HR/FB volatility
- Landen Roupp (SP): 50.1 IP, 0.5 fWAR — late-season call-up, solid FIP relative to ERA

The gap between Tier 1 and Tier 2 is the defining structural characteristic of the 2024 staff. Webb accounted for a disproportionate share of innings and value; the rotation had no second ace to absorb workload when Webb rested.

## 2024 Bullpen Construction

The bullpen featured one elite arm and significant volatility elsewhere:

- Ryan Walker (RP): 80.0 IP, 1.7 fWAR — most valuable reliever, deceptive delivery
- Tyler Rogers (RP): 70.1 IP, 0.6 fWAR — submarine specialist, extreme GB%, exceptional command
- Camilo Doval (CL): 59.0 IP, 23 SV, 0.1 fWAR — triple-digit sinker, ERA/xERA gap suggests strand rate regression

Doval's surface ERA (4.88) and xERA (3.32) diverged sharply — one of the most analytically interesting cases on the staff. His underlying contact quality was closer to average than his ERA implied, but strand rate collapse inflated runs allowed.

## Structural Risks

**Single-ace dependency**: Webb's 204.2 IP and 4.4 WAR represent a concentration of value unusual even for contending staffs. If Webb misses starts, there is no comparable fallback.

**Home/road split**: Oracle Park's park factor meaningfully suppresses ERA for Giants starters. Road performance is a consistent regression point; analysts should always check park-adjusted metrics (FIP, xFIP) alongside raw ERA when evaluating road splits.

**HR/FB vulnerability in Tier 2**: Harrison (12.3% HR/FB) and Birdsong (15.1% HR/FB) both ran elevated home run rates. Oracle Park partially masks this at home, but road starts expose it directly.

**Strand rate fragility**: LOB% is one of the noisiest pitching stats year to year. Walker (82.9% LOB%) and Doval (low LOB%) are both candidates for mean reversion in opposite directions.
