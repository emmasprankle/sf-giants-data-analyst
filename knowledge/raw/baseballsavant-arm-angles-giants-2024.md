## Metric Definition

Arm angle is measured at the moment of pitch release using Hawk-Eye camera tracking. Center of mass is normalized in the leaderboard visualization — it does not imply every pitcher is standing in the same place on the mound.

Scale reference:
- **Negative (< 0°):** Submarine — ball released below shoulder height
- **0–20°:** Low three-quarter / low sidearm slot
- **20–40°:** Three-quarter arm slot (most common)
- **40–60°:** High three-quarter / approaching over-the-top
- **60–90°:** Over-the-top

## Analytical Significance for Giants Staff

**Tyler Rogers (-64°):** The most extreme arm angle on the staff — full submarine delivery. The extreme below-horizontal release point creates a deceptive upward angle on the ball as seen by right-handed hitters, generating both the platoon split (far less effective vs. LHH) and the elite GB% (54.5%). This is a mechanical explanation for an outcome that ERA and FIP don't capture.

**Walker (17°) and Doval (19°):** Both pitch from a low three-quarter slot. Low arm angles generate natural downward and arm-side run on sinkers and two-seamers, which explains both pitchers' above-average GB rates (Walker 46.9%, Doval 60.0%) without needing to invoke anything about pitch quality or command.

**Logan Webb (22°):** Webb's low arm slot is the mechanical foundation of his sinker. The 22° release angle naturally produces the downward plane and arm-side run that generates his 56.8% GB% — the low slot is why the sinker works, not just that he throws it.

**Jordan Hicks (29°):** Slightly higher slot than Webb; his sinker generates less horizontal run but more velocity. The slot difference partially explains why Hicks' ground ball rate (50.9%) is real but lower than Webb's.

**Hayden Birdsong (41°):** Among the highest arm slots on the staff. A high arm slot generates more four-seam backspin and less downward plane, which is why Birdsong has the highest HR/FB rate (15.1%) on the staff — his arm slot works against ground ball induction.

**Blake Snell (57°):** Very high slot for a lefty, which generates extreme vertical break on his curveball and makes his fastball harder to lift. High slots from left-handers are particularly difficult for right-handed hitters because the release point appears from a sharp angle.
